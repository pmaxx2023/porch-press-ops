### C. What happened (wartime fate + when he died)

**Answer:**
- **Wartime conflict:** Hospital register **D007** lists Elias M. Corbett, Co. C, 36th Indiana, Nashville, admitted 1863-10-20, status **dead of disease** on 1863-11-12. Company muster **D008** (≈1863-12-01, after leaving convalescence Nashville) lists **Eli Corbett**, same unit, status **deserted / missing — supposed dead**. Those two military lines disagree; the muster is consistent with leaving convalescence and being carried as missing/presumed dead rather than a confirmed company-death.
- **Decoy check:** **D015** (Silas M. Corbit, Co. B 83rd Indiana, Dearborn Co., dead of disease 1863-11-09) is a similar-name hospital death, **not** this soldier (wrong given name spelling/unit/residence).
- **Postwar life:** He resurfaces as head of household with Lydia in Jefferson County on **D010** (1870, surname spelling “Corbit”) and **D011** (1880, “Eli Corbett”), with the children named on Lydia’s claim.
- **Actual death:** **1887-02-14**, pneumonia, Madison area / Jefferson County (**D004**); burial/death extract **D013** (burial 1887-02-16) again names widow **Lydia Corbett**.

Working reconstruction: he did **not** die in the 1863 hospital line as Martha was told; he left convalescence, was carried deserted/missing–supposed dead, later lived as “widower” with Lydia in Jefferson, and died of pneumonia in **1887**.

**Exhibit citations:** D004, D007, D008, D010, D011, D013, D015  
**Confidence:** **high** on 1887 death and postwar cohabitation; **high/medium** on desertion/survival reading of D007 vs D008 (strong conflict + postwar schedules make wartime death untenable)
