# 2. PLAYER-FACING SUMMARY (no spoilers)

**Cold open (first pages):** Two women have filed Civil War **widow’s pension** claims for the same enlisted man. The bureau papers are already in conflict. You are not reading a family history chapter — you are walking into an open dispute.

Period marriages, musters, censuses, death papers, and affidavits appear to back **both** claims. The records disagree the way witnesses disagree.

The player must use the supplied records to determine:

1. which claim is **legally / historically supportable** from the paper;
2. what **each woman asserted** (and what a reasonable reader of her file would believe);
3. what **actually happened** to the soldier and the two households — without treating any one record as omniscient truth.

Solving means an evidence-supported adjudication of the dispute, not finding a hidden cipher. When you finish, the case resolves — and the series tone should make you want the next title.

# 3. CORE EXPERIENCE / WHAT COUNTS AS SOLVING

## The player investigates

- Who the soldier was (identity continuity across name variants and rolls)
- Whether / how he left the war (death vs desertion vs other — **records conflict**)
- The earlier marriage and whether it was ever dissolved
- The later marriage and what that ceremony’s paperwork claims
- Parallel household reconstruction (censuses, children’s births, residence)
- Death / burial naming practices vs marital law
- Pension affidavits as **assertions**, not facts

## Solving = the player can support all of the following from the open record set

| Solution element | Player must establish |
| --- | --- |
| **A. Supportable widow** | Which claimant’s marriage is the undissolved prior union that controls widow status |
| **B. Claimant beliefs** | What each woman believed or asserted in her file (sincere vs knowingly false is a separate layer) |
| **C. What happened** | The soldier’s wartime fate as reconstructed from conflicting military paper + postwar life |
| **D. Why both claims look real** | Which documents legitimately support each file without collapsing “record says X” into “X happened” |

## Explicitly not required for “solved”

- Knowing every private motive
- Modern legal advice; this is **period-record adjudication** for a genealogy mystery
- The sealed ending document (see §8)

## Fail / incomplete if

- Player picks a widow solely because the death certificate names her
- Player conflates “ceremony happened” with “marriage was lawful”
- Player treats a single muster line as definitive without addressing the conflicting roll

# 4. CANONICAL ANSWER (internal; Canon builds the ledger from this)

## One-sentence truth

**Private Elias M. Corbett deserted after a wartime hospital stretch that left contradictory “dead” / “deserted” paper; his first marriage to Martha Jane Ashby was never dissolved; he later married Lydia Ann Briggs as a supposed widower; when he died, both women filed sincere-looking widow claims — but only Martha’s undissolved prior marriage is supportable.**

## Principals (Canon assigns IDs / exact vitals)

### Soldier — Elias M. Corbett
- Enlisted as Private, Company C, **36th Indiana Infantry** (Aug 1862)
- Married **Martha Jane Ashby**, **12 Mar 1858**, Ripley County, Indiana
- Children with Martha before / early in the war (Canon: exactly **two** surviving into the pension era)
- Late 1863: hospitalized (Nashville / convalescent context — Canon picks specific hospital consistent with the regiment)
- **Record conflict (required mechanic):**
  - One military / hospital-adjacent record line marks him **dead of disease** (date ~late 1863)
  - Company descriptive / muster trail later marks him **deserted** (or “missing — supposed dead”) after leaving convalescence
- Truth: he **deserted**, recovered under his own or slight variant name (**Eli Corbett** on at least one postwar record), and never returned to Martha
- Remarried **Lydia Ann Briggs**, **4 Jun 1866**, Jefferson County, Indiana, claiming widower status
- Lived with Lydia until death: **14 Feb 1887**, Jefferson County (pneumonia or equivalent ordinary cause)
- Burial / death register names **Lydia** as widow / informant wife

### Claimant A — Martha Jane (Ashby) Corbett
- First / lawful wife
- Remains in Ripley County sphere; **does not remarry**
- Receives informal wartime word that Elias died; builds life as a soldier’s widow
- Files widow’s pension citing 1858 marriage + wartime death
- Her file is strengthened by early marriage register + children’s continuity + no divorce found
- She does **not** need to be villainous; she is acting on incomplete information plus correct prior marriage

### Claimant B — Lydia Ann (Briggs) Corbett
- Ceremony wife; socially and domestically the widow of record at death
- Believes she is the lawful widow (Elias told her his first wife died of fever before they met)
- Files widow’s pension citing 1866 marriage + 1887 death certificate naming her
- Her file is strengthened by long cohabitation, children of the second household, census “wife,” death paper
- She is **not** required to know about Martha during the marriage (Canon default: she learns of the conflict only when pension files collide)

## Legal / historical supportability (period-mystery standard)

| Question | Canonical answer |
| --- | --- |
| Was the 1858 marriage real? | Yes |
| Was it ever dissolved (divorce / lawful remarriage of Martha)? | **No** — no divorce on file; Martha never remarried |
| Was Elias actually dead in 1863? | **No** — desertion after conflicting “dead” paperwork |
| Was the 1866 ceremony real? | Yes (ceremony + domestic marriage) |
| Was the 1866 marriage lawful? | **No** — prior spouse living; undissolved prior marriage |
| Who is the supportable widow for claim purposes? | **Martha Jane (Ashby) Corbett** |
| Does that erase Lydia’s lived marriage? | No — the packet must honor that records of lived marriage are real even when the union is not lawful |

## Emotional answer (not a crime twist)

The case is not “catch the bigamist.” It is:

> **Two widows. One undissolved marriage. Paper that tells both stories — and lies about the war.**

The player should feel the weight of:
- Martha’s correct legal claim built on incomplete wartime knowledge;
- Lydia’s authentic domestic claim built on a lie she was told;
- Elias’s desertion as the hinge that created both files.

# 5. RECORD VS TRUTH (hard rule for this case)

Maintain two layers everywhere Canon and Trail work:

| Layer | Meaning | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Canonical truth** | What happened | Elias deserted; Martha’s marriage never ended; Lydia was lied to |
| **Recorded observation** | What a document asserts | Hospital line says dead; death register says Lydia is widow; 1866 license says widower |

**Never** let document generation “fix” the truth. A death certificate naming Lydia is evidence of naming practice and household standing, not proof that Martha’s marriage ended.

Required noise (ordinary, not clues by themselves):
- One age off by ~1 year on a census
- One surname variant / ditto quirk (`Corbett` / `Corbit` / `Eli` vs `Elias`)
- One relationship label that is socially true and legally incomplete (`wife` on census for Lydia)

# 7. OPENING ARTIFACTS / COLD OPEN (Trail refines; Brief intent)

**Cold-open rule:** the first pages must feel like the first five minutes of a *48 Hours* episode — stakes first, context later.

Player starts with roughly **3–4** items that establish the dispute without solving it:

1. **Pension conflict cover / bureau note** — two widow claims, same soldier, both pending / contested (**immediate stakes**)  
2. **Martha’s claim excerpt** — asserts 1858 marriage + soldier’s wartime death (**witness A**)  
3. **Lydia’s claim excerpt** — asserts 1866 marriage + 1887 death as his widow (**witness B**)  
4. **One “neutral” hook** — e.g. 1887 death register naming Lydia **or** a muster card with the ambiguous fate line (Trail picks which opens cleaner)

First-page ban list: county gazetteer essays, regiment history lectures, multi-generation pedigree dumps, “how to research” manuals before the conflict is felt.

Do **not** hand the player a divorce search result or sealed confession up front.

# 8. PROVEABLE VS SEALED REVEAL

## Proveable from open records (required for solve)

- 1858 marriage exists and predates 1866
- No divorce / dissolution located for Martha & Elias
- Martha never remarried
- Military paper conflicts on dead vs deserted
- Elias appears alive in postwar records tied to Lydia
- 1866 ceremony happened; Lydia lived as wife until his death
- Supportable widow = Martha; second marriage unlawful though domestically real
- Each woman’s affidavit claims are understandable from her information set

## Sealed ending (after submit; not required to solve)

A short private document (brother’s affidavit, comrade letter, or Elias note fragment) that confirms:

- he left convalescence and did not return to the regiment;
- he knew Martha was living when he married Lydia;
- he told Lydia his first wife had died.

This supplies motive/knowledge color. Players who already adjudicated correctly should feel confirmation, not a new legal theory.

**Series rule:** the sealed beat closes *this* case. It must not invent a dangling second mystery that only the next purchase can answer. Appetite for the next title comes from craft and tone, not from an unfinished adjudication.

---

# 9. DECOYS / ELIMINATION UNIVERSE

Trail designs the research graph; Brief locks these decoy classes:

1. **Same-name soldier noise** — at least one other Corbett / Corbit in Indiana rolls who *did* die in hospital (must be eliminable by company, age, or residence)
2. **Remarriage red herring** — a neighboring widow who remarried and lost/regained pension eligibility (teaches the rule without being our Martha)
3. **False “divorce found” temptation** — a divorce in the right county/year for a **different** couple with similar names (must not cleanly match Elias + Martha)
4. **Death-certificate primacy trap** — documents that make Lydia look like the only possible widow if the player never checks the earlier marriage

Elimination is of **wrong adjudications and wrong identities**, not of twelve infants (Baby’s mechanic).

# 10. ACT STRUCTURE / BINGEABLE SOLUTION PATH (Trail will detail)

Design as **escalating reveals**, not a flat folder of exhibits. Each act ends with a payoff that changes what the player thinks they know.

## Act I — Both claims look real (cold open)
1. Open the dual claim → map what each woman asserts  
2. Verify both marriages as recorded events  

**Payoff:** “This isn’t a clerical mix-up. Two real wedding papers.”

## Act II — The war paper splits
3. Rebuild Martha’s wartime household → she never remarries  
4. Confront military dead-vs-deserted conflict  

**Payoff:** “You thought you knew how he died. The muster doesn’t agree with the hospital line.”

## Act III — He’s alive in the paper
5. Find Elias alive postwar with Lydia  

**Payoff:** “The ‘dead’ soldier is in the census with another wife.”

## Act IV — Adjudication
6. Search for divorce / dissolution → none for this couple  
7. Weigh death naming vs prior undissolved marriage  
8. Adjudicate: Martha supportable; Lydia’s file is domestically true and legally secondary  

**Payoff:** “The death certificate isn’t the marriage law.”

## Close — Satisfying ending (not a cliffhanger)
9. Submit → sealed confirmation of desertion + the lie told to Lydia  

**Payoff:** This case resolves cleanly. Sealed color deepens motive; it does not reopen a new unsolved mystery. Series appetite comes from tone and craft — “I want another one of these” — not from withholding the answer.

## Investigation rhythm (Trail / Binder)

- Short research loops: ask → pull 1–3 records → update working theory → next ask  
- Every loop needs a visible payoff (confirm, eliminate, or force a revise)  
- Avoid one giant undifferentiated archive dump  
- Hint ladder exists for stuck players without flattening the binge  

Key player moments:

- “Both marriages are real events.”  
- “The war paper doesn’t agree with itself.”  
- “He’s in the census with the second wife.”  
- “There’s no divorce.”  
- “The death certificate isn’t the marriage law.”  
- “I finished — and I want the next case.”
