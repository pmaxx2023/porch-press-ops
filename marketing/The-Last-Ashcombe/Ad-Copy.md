# The Last Ashcombe - three finished ad concepts

All three image files are finished square creatives. Scenes are illustrative artwork, not photographs of the exact printed contents. The preview PDF contains actual selected product pages. No campaign has been posted or funded. Add the final product link only after publication.

## 01-the-house.png - the premise

**Headline:** Who inherits the house?

**Caption:** Julian Ashcombe is dead. Three relatives want the keys. A family settlement and a box of records stand between their claims and the truth. In The Last Ashcombe, you reconstruct the family and prove who inherits.

**Short description:** A printable genealogy and succession mystery set in a fictional 1913 estate.

**Call to action:** Open the archive. Prove the heir.

**Alternative short caption:** Three petitions. Sixteen exhibits. One rightful heir. What can you prove?

## 02-prove-the-claim.png - the investigative challenge

**Headline:** A family name is not a claim.

**Caption:** Compare the names. Check the dates. Follow the branches. The Last Ashcombe puts the family records and the succession rules in your hands. Your task is to support the winning claim with evidence and explain why the competing claims fall behind.

**Short description:** Sixteen exhibits, a family tree to reconstruct, and a decision you must defend.

**Call to action:** Follow the records. Prove the heir.

**Alternative short caption:** Read the settlement. Challenge the petitions. Find the person the evidence supports.

## 03-investigate-tonight.png - the at-home experience

**Headline:** Tonight, settle an inheritance.

**Caption:** Clear a table, take a pencil, and open the Ashcombe papers. Investigate alone or compare theories with someone beside you. Everything you need is in the packet: the rules, the records, the worksheets, and sealed help if you need it.

**Short description:** Print-and-play investigation. No host or outside research required.

**Call to action:** Print. Trace. Prove.

**Alternative short caption:** A quiet table. A disputed estate. An answer you can prove.
