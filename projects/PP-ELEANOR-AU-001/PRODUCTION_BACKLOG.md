### 3 — Experience design

Design the case portal and each archive as a credible editorial system. Required review frames:

- portal: opening, active search mission, evidence locker, cast reveal, claim ruling, episode map, final commit, recovery;
- archives: home, catalog/list, record, image/transcript, about/provenance, 404;
- breakpoints: 390×844 mobile and a desktop viewport;
- states: loading, invalid token, duplicate token, unavailable episode, import error, offline/recovery, reduced motion.

The five investigator identities need distinct visual rhythms without becoming generation costumes. Synthetic status must be legible in onboarding and About copy.

### 4 — Controlled archive universe

Build the approved records as static, readable, stable pages. A required record is complete only when:

- its human-readable page, media, transcript, accession code, dates, provenance, and related links agree;
- search, citation, and direct recovery routes work;
- archive voice and design differ from the other institutions;
- essential evidence remains available without JavaScript;
- no clue appears earlier than its release tier;
- no page or asset exposes the solution.

Proposed launch topology, pending infrastructure review:

- one neutral controlled root domain;
- one subdomain or separately deployed surface per fictional institution;
- one separately branded case portal;
- a single private source monorepo or equivalent controlled deployment origin;
- ordinary links and independent sitemaps for discovery;
- no required third-party evidence.

Use existing no-cost preview infrastructure for development. Domain purchase, DNS changes, and public indexing require later explicit approval.

### 5 — Portal and showrunner

Implement:

- versioned local case state;
- exact token normalization and validation;
- player notes that remain local;
- evidence detail and source-link history;
- supported/contested/disproved claim rulings;
- deterministic scene conditions, priority, one-time play, and replay;
- episode gates and progressive hints;
- JSON export/import with schema validation and safe migration;
- explicit reset with export reminder;
- structured final reconstruction;
- no ingestion of external-agent conversations.

Keep cast copy and trigger conditions in release-tier packets. A scene can react to verified state but cannot silently add an artifact or settle the player's theory.

### 6 — Sealed finale

Use a narrow server-side boundary:

- input: case version, normalized structured answers, and evidence classes;
- output: insufficient-evidence guidance or one approved resolution branch;
- private payload: scoring keys, capstone record, solution, and aftermath;
- exclusions: free-form notes, outside-agent chat, personal identifiers, or unnecessary analytics.

Rate-limit enumeration, suppress solution-bearing logs, and keep private payloads out of public build artifacts and source control. Provide an owner-only local fallback for emergency customer support.

### 7 — Audit

Run the `audit-agentic-unfiction` release matrix. Required result families:

1. data/schema and dependency validation;
2. semantic proof review;
3. URL/link/media/transcript crawl;
4. episode/showrunner state coverage;
5. visual/accessibility/performance review;
6. repository, history, asset-metadata, bundle, endpoint, and search spoiler scan;
7. clean-state desktop/mobile completion;
8. rollback and support-path test.

Any missing route, early scene, public answer, non-unique factual close, inaccessible document, or inconsistent record is a release hold.

### 8 — Blind testing

Run at least:

- three human solo tests unfamiliar with the solution;
- two complete playthroughs using different player-supplied web agents where available;
- one no-search-index playthrough using only citations and recovery;
- one mobile-first playthrough;
- one accessibility-focused keyboard and screen-reader pass.

Observe rather than coach. Record discovery failures, unsupported agent claims, hint use, incorrect theories, emotional peaks, dead time, completion, and qualitative enjoyment. Only these sessions may support duration and difficulty claims.

### 9 — Commerce candidate

Default product hypothesis: combined edition including Episode Zero plus the agentic season. Owner_Bot must approve final title, price, promise, fiction disclosure, device/agent requirements, privacy language, customer support path, preview images, and delivery contents.

Grok then creates an unpublished candidate and verifies:

- exact product/variant and preserved legacy assets;
- no stale duration/difficulty claims;
- fiction disclosure before payment;
- correct entry URL and backup start guide;
- digital delivery and test order;
- mobile product page, cart, checkout boundary, and fulfillment;
- no live navigation/hero change before release approval.

### 10 — Release and rollback

Release requires one Owner_Bot decision naming the exact build, product, theme/template, URL set, price, and publication time. Capture pre-change state first.

Minimum first-day monitoring:

- archive and portal availability;
- broken evidence routes;
- sealed-envelope errors;
- customer access/fulfillment failures;
- actual human starts versus bot traffic;
- support reports indicating confusion with a real missing-person case.

Rollback means restoring the prior Shopify availability/template state and unpublishing the new entry path; it must not require deleting archives, customer data, or source history.
