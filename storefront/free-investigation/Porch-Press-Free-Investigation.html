The solution
The person beneath the memorial

It was his mother.

Miriam Cross is the documented occupant of G17. Roscoe’s name was a memorial. Her burial came later, with Edith’s permission.

  1. Follow the delivery. The interment entry R04 gives I-431, account U-572, and seal S44. R05 connects all three to Miriam Cross, delivered to G17. Mabel’s separate account is U-573, seal S45, at North Field B6.
  2. Connect the family. Roscoe’s marriage record, R13, names his mother as “Miriam Cross, formerly Hale, born Dale.”
  3. Separate a memorial from a burial. R07 orders a stone and foundation, with no remains delivered. R08 records no identifiable human remains recovered from the loss of the Mercy.
  4. Read the request. In R14, Miriam asks to be buried in G17 while Roscoe’s name stays on the stone. Edith’s permission authorizes that burial under U-572. R02 distinguishes the stone reference S-64 from interment I-431.
“He should not lose his place in the world because the sea did not send him home.”

Miriam’s letter to Edith · R14

A name preserved. Another overlooked.

The records support an authorized burial beneath an existing memorial. They do not require a coffin swap or a false burial entry. They also do not establish Roscoe’s exact fate, the location of his remains, or why Ada was not told.
