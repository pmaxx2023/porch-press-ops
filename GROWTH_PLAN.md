**Porch Press — Extreme Growth Plan**

Prepared September 6, 2026 · Owner_Bot · 90-day execution proposal

**The central bet**

Porch Press can produce mysteries faster than it can currently acquire customers. The next growth investment should turn the existing catalog into a repeatable source of attention, trial, purchases, and recommendations. Publishing more full cases without distribution would enlarge the inventory while leaving the commercial problem intact.

Build an audience around the pleasure of uncovering a family secret. Give people a small, satisfying investigation in public; sell the deeper investigation; make the next case easy to discover; equip creators and clubs to introduce the experience to their audiences.

Proposed public positioning: **“Family secrets. Paper trails. Your investigation.”** Supporting explanation: “Fictional mysteries you solve through family records. Download, print, and investigate.” Keep the digital format explicit. The internal creative references remain internal.

The stretch objective is **1,000 distinct paying customers within 90 days**, with no additional owner capital beyond the existing $100. This is an ambition with demanding prerequisites, not a forecast. The first commercial proof remains one person outside PM’s personal circle purchasing a complete case at its approved full price. Owner tests and free copies do not count.

This deliverable is a plan. New offers, commission agreements, outreach, email sequences, and public content below are proposals; they have not been launched by preparing this document. Existing authorizations and the current campaign’s release conditions continue to govern.

**2. Choose a narrow entry point with room to expand**

The initial customer hypothesis is an adult who enjoys family mysteries and evidence-based investigation, and wants an activity they can start at home. Test that motivation before assuming genealogy expertise, a particular gender, or a particular age band predicts purchases.

| Audience hypothesis | Promise to test | Demonstration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mystery and investigative-story readers | “Follow the records and reach your own conclusion.” | One brief evidence comparison followed by the full case offer. |
| Family-history enthusiasts | “Use the research skills you enjoy to solve a fictional family secret.” | A surname, household, or identity deduction. |
| People seeking a shared activity | “A mystery you can investigate together.” | Actual pages on a table and an example of discussing evidence. |

Lead with the first audience; use the second as a focused partner channel. Treat date-night framing as a later message test. Keep the existing Meta US 18+ broad audience unchanged during its first experiment; these content hypotheses are not instructions to create three paid ad sets.

The competitive category already contains printable cases, hints, and solo/group investigation. Those features explain the product but are weak differentiation by themselves. Print Mysteries publicly sells that format. Porch Press’s stronger hypothesis is emotionally charged family identity, belonging, inheritance, and conflicting accounts, supported by fair deductions. [Print Mysteries](https://printmysteries.com/)

**3. Build one path from attention to purchase**

Use a consistent sequence: a public clue invites participation; an optional free miniature lets the visitor experience the method; Baby in a Basket offers a deeper investigation; a three-case collection increases the purchase value; a later case recommendation encourages a second order.

Keep direct purchase available throughout. A visitor already interested in Baby should be able to buy without subscribing or playing the free miniature.

The free miniature should be one newly authored, self-contained case: roughly three evidence items, one meaningful deduction, a hint, and an answer. A design target of five to ten minutes is reasonable, but do not advertise a playtime until observed. Its conclusion must satisfy the reader. It must not reveal a paid case’s decisive clue or ending.

Let people attempt the first clue on the page without an email gate. Offer the printable miniature plus a clearly described weekly clue email through an optional signup. The signup must save to a verified destination. A vote or answer submission is not email consent.

Free mini cases are an established category tactic: Unsolved Case Files offers a playable mini case alongside paid-case links and an email signup. That supports testing the mechanism; it supplies no forecast of Porch Press’s conversion. [Unsolved Case Files mini case](https://www.unsolvedcasefiles.com/madeline.html)

**5. Make the content itself enjoyable**

Create six original public pieces per week, then adapt those same pieces to supported channels. Start with Instagram as the primary social surface. Produce Pinterest versions from suitable existing work after access is established. Add another short-video channel only if republishing adds negligible work and an accessible posting route exists.

| Weekly piece | Number | What the audience does | Buying connection |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Tiny evidence challenge | 3 | Compare two or three records and make a deduction. | Link to the free miniature or relevant case. |
| Character dilemma | 1 | Discuss a human question without exposing a paid ending. | Demonstrate the emotional experience. |
| Product demonstration | 1 | See real pages, printing choices, and how hints work. | Remove uncertainty about the download. |
| Answer and explanation | 1 | See how the public clues support a conclusion. | Earn trust and invite a deeper case. |

Suggested creative starting points, to be checked against canonical content before publication:

- Baby identity hook, already approved: “Mabel knew where she was found. She never knew who her parents were.”
- Baby investigation hook, already approved: “A basket in a church pew. A missing family history. Can you trace Mabel’s beginnings?”
- Product demonstration: “What do you actually do with a printable genealogy mystery?” Show the start instructions, one spoiler-safe record, and the closed hints/solution files.
- Standalone sample: “Two records. One woman. What connects them?” Author an original miniature that genuinely supports that question.
- Character discussion: “What makes someone family?” Connect to the verified premise without turning opinion into an evidentiary answer.
- Reveal: “Here is the detail that connects the two records.” Explain only the public miniature’s solution.

Use actual approved previews when demonstrating the paid product. Label new fictional promotional records as sample material. Do not fabricate customer reactions, reviews, or difficulty claims. Public challenges must reward careful reading; hiding necessary information to provoke comments damages the promise of fair investigation.

Publish in two prepared weekly batches. Track the complete journey from the content to purchases. Likes, saves, and comments help identify interest; they do not establish sales demand. After twelve pieces, concentrate on the two formats producing the most qualified visits and useful customer responses, while treating the comparison as exploratory.

**6. Borrow distribution through useful collaborations**

The highest-upside low-capital tactic is to give a relevant creator or community an enjoyable activity they can share, supported by a paid product their audience might want. The proposal must be more useful than “please promote my store.”

Target genealogy educators, family-history newsletters, mystery reviewers, small book communities, and puzzle/activity creators. Prioritize evidence that their audience actually participates in record analysis or mystery discussions. Follower count alone is insufficient.

Work toward 150 researched prospects over the sprint. Start with 20 carefully qualified prospects per week and a maximum of 10 individually tailored invitations per week after outreach is authorized. Expand to 20 only when the first messages produce relevant replies. Use each prospect’s stated business contact route. One follow-up after roughly a week is sufficient unless they engage.

For every candidate, record the actual channel, recent relevant example, official contact route, proposed audience activity, invitation status, publication, traffic, and sales. This is one simple working table, not a new CRM build.

Offer a small pilot package: a review copy, a ready-to-publish standalone clue, its answer explanation, approved product visuals, and an optional affiliate arrangement. The creator can assess the product independently; a review copy does not purchase a positive review. Credit gifted products and commercial relationships plainly in the resulting content.

Proposed affiliate terms for the first five partners: 30% of attributable product revenue after discounts and refunds, excluding tax. No upfront sponsorship payment. Reserve commissions when earned and pay through the agreed supported process. Define attribution and refund handling before accepting a partner, including how conflicting referrals are resolved. Do not promise reliable attribution from a UTM string alone.

A $24.99 collection generates about $7.50 commission before rounding adjustments and refunds. A $9.99 case generates about $3.00. These small amounts may be insufficient for larger creators; focus on hosts whose audience activity and editorial interest make the collaboration worthwhile.

Use existing supported tracking if it can actually attribute purchases. Shopify Collabs is a possible implementation after checking merchant/creator eligibility and billing. It tracks referral links or codes, processes commissions through Shopify billing, and charges 2.9% on automatic commission payments. This is a fee on the commission, not on total order revenue. Do not describe it as costless or assume it is already installed. [Shopify Collabs payments](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/promoting-marketing/collabs/merchants/payments)

After three partners actually publish, prepare a coordinated public clue week with them. Supply a complete sample challenge and answer; each partner has a distinct source link. Use one common destination and one clear paid offer. This is the first potential distribution multiplier. A list of partners who accepted free files is not a working channel.

**7. Turn completed play into the next purchase**

Propose a short email sequence for subscribers: welcome and miniature immediately; a hint/answer route two days later; a deeper-case invitation on day four; a final format/help explanation on day seven. Then send one worthwhile public clue per week. Keep answers available on the site so receiving marketing is not required to finish an already-started sample.

For buyers, send fulfillment through the established delivery path. A later support check should establish whether they could open the files and begin. Offer another case after a reasonable play window, suppress titles already owned, and distinguish a purchase from a completion report. Do not infer that someone finished because a week elapsed.

Ask for a concrete response: “Which record changed your mind?” and “Where did you get stuck?” Request permission before using a quotation publicly. Offer a spoiler-safe share card and a link to the free miniature, rather than encouraging redistribution of paid PDFs.

Shopify Messaging currently includes up to 10,000 manual or automated emails per month free; additional email volume is usage-priced. Verify the store’s configuration and monitor total sends. A list of 2,000 subscribers receiving four weekly messages plus welcome sequences can approach that allowance. No new paid email subscription is necessary for the initial pilot. [Shopify Messaging pricing](https://www.shopify.com/email-marketing)

**8. Use economics to decide what can scale**

The following is a deliberately simplified planning model, not the store’s actual fee schedule. Assume payment/platform charges of 5% of order value plus $0.30 and a further 5% cash reserve for refunds. Customer-paid taxes are excluded from revenue. Obtain actual processor charges, refunds, and support costs before a spending decision.

| Order | Revenue | Contribution before acquisition under these assumptions | Proposed acquisition target: retain half that contribution | After 30% partner commission, before any payout fee |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| One case | $9.99 | $8.69 | CAC at or below $4.35 | $5.69 |
| Three-case collection | $24.99 | $22.19 | CAC at or below $11.10 | $14.69 |
| Illustrative blended order | $14.99 | $13.19 | CAC at or below $6.60 | $8.69 |

The $14.99 average requires one third of orders to be $24.99 collections and two thirds to be $9.99 singles. That mix is a hypothesis. Under the same model, Collabs’ 2.9% fee on a 30% commission reduces contribution by approximately $0.09 per single or $0.22 per collection, before any other applicable charges.

CAC means compatible acquisition spending divided by acquired paying customers. It is undefined when there are no customers. Separate paid advertising CAC, partner acquisition cost including commission, and blended acquisition cost. Attribute commissions as expenses when earned even if payout is later. Track labor separately; cash-light distribution still consumes work.

At a hypothetical $0.50 click cost and 2% click-to-purchase rate, paid acquisition costs $25 per order. The single case cannot sustain that. With the assumed $8.69 single-case contribution, break-even CPC at 2% conversion is only about $0.17; preserving half the contribution requires about $0.09. A $1 click would need approximately 11.5% conversion merely to cover acquisition from that single case’s modeled contribution.

This is why the plan combines free distribution, commissions funded by actual sales, and bundles. Do not justify a loss on the first order using an imagined lifetime value. Only observed repeat-purchase contribution can support that calculation.

The model excludes existing subscriptions and owner labor. Its result is incremental contribution, not whole-business profit.

**10. Sequence the experiments**

Run one material experiment at a time within each scarce traffic source. Organic publishing and partner preparation can continue while the paid test is blocked; they should not alter the paid offer midway through its run.

| Experiment | Decision | Measurement and stopping rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Existing paid pilot | Does the current Baby offer produce purchases within the bounded test? | Purchases, contribution, outbound clicks, arrivals, checkout starts. End at scheduled finish or all-in cap; stop immediately for broken offer, delivery, or tracking. |
| First 12 public pieces | Which format earns relevant visits and useful engagement? | Source-tagged arrivals, sample use where observable, purchases. Organic comparison is directional; algorithms and timing confound results. |
| Free miniature pilot | Does experiencing a deduction lead to deeper interest? | Verified signup, answer-page reach or declared completion, later paid purchase. These are distinct events; downloads do not equal completion. |
| Collection introduction | Does the additional offer improve revenue and contribution per visitor? | Record product-page version and purchase mix. Sequential before/after data is exploratory, not causal proof. Do not rerun the paid pilot with an unrecorded offer change. |
| First five partners | Can relevant partners generate purchases economically? | Publication, actual visits, attributable orders, commission, net contribution. Evaluate after agreed publication dates; pause recruitment if recipients do not publish. |

If a later question merits confirmation, calculate sample requirements from the observed baseline and the smallest worthwhile improvement. Predefine the window and allocation. Equal budgets or simultaneous ads are not random assignment. Use honest result labels: directional leader, inconclusive, invalid measurement, or supported winner only when the design supports it.

For planning, one first order per new customer at a $14.99 average yields $14,990 gross from 1,000 customers. Repeat orders would be additional; they are not assumed. At a 2% session-to-first-order rate, that requires about 50,000 eligible sessions. At 1%, it requires 100,000; at 3%, about 33,334. These rates are assumptions, not benchmarks or measured Porch Press performance.

One possible distribution requirement is 25,000 sessions from partners, 20,000 from Porch Press’s own social activity, and 5,000 from search or other relevant placements. At the assumed 2% rate, these correspond to 500, 400, and 100 first orders. Do not count email return visits as additional unique people or assign one order to multiple channels.

That is a demanding target: twenty publishing partners would need to deliver an average of 1,250 sessions each to meet the partner component. If social posts generated site visits at an assumed 1% of views, 20,000 sessions would require about two million views. These are reverse calculations showing the scale required, not audience commitments or expected reach.

The most plausible upside comes from a public clue format people repeatedly share, or a few highly relevant communities that repeatedly feature the cases. Neither exists as proven evidence today. If those mechanisms fail to emerge, reset the forecast rather than inventing a viral multiplier.

Around days 30–60, run the coordinated clue week with partners who have already published. Around days 60–90, concentrate on the proven case/offer combination and relevant seasonal activity. With a September start, autumn/Halloween provides a possible occasion; use the existing fictional stories without recasting them as party kits or promising new releases to meet a calendar.

**14. Apply concrete stop and change rules**

| Observed problem | Next action |
| --- | --- |
| Little relevant traffic | Change distribution, creator selection, or the public hook. Do not commission more full mysteries. |
| Views and comments, almost no destination visits | Make the next action and product connection clearer; inspect actual link availability. |
| Visits but little sample use or checkout activity | Review promise, product demonstration, and format friction with actual visitors. |
| Checkout activity without purchases | Inspect payment, total price, device behavior, and trust questions; distinguish abandoned sessions from tracking gaps. |
| Purchases but poor delivery or repeated confusion | Pause promotion of the affected offer and fix the failure before adding traffic. |
| Free sample engagement but few purchases | Test a stronger paid-case connection or direct product route; do not count email growth as commercial validation. |
| Partner invitations accepted but no publications | Reduce review friction and agree on a specific optional activity; stop sending more free files until the bottleneck is understood. |
| Paid cap reached with no orders | Stop spending, label the result honestly, and diagnose. No automatic $50 retry. |
| A few profitable paid orders | Reconcile fees and replicate within another explicit cap. Do not jump to a large daily budget. |
| Twenty real paid customers reveal recurring product defects | Prioritize the defects. More exposure would amplify them. |

A no-sale window is not automatically a failed business. The strength of that conclusion depends on traffic quantity, relevance, measurement, offer stability, and the buyer path. A positive social reaction is equally insufficient to establish a business.

Existing cases supply the paid inventory. Do not add a ninth full case simply to maintain production momentum.

No new recurring tools, purchased followers, broad giveaways, generic bulk outreach, full storefront redesign, or simultaneous launches on every marketplace. A new channel earns its place by producing relevant visits or solving a specific bottleneck with low incremental work.
